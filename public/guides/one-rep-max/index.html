Estimate your one rep max

You do not need to test a true max to know roughly where it is. A hard set of 3 to 10 reps gives a useful estimate.

Updated September 2026

1RM calculator

The formulas

  • Epley: 1RM = weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30)
  • Brzycki: 1RM = weight × 36 ÷ (37 − reps)

Both give close answers in the 3 to 10 rep range and drift apart above that. The calculator shows both and uses their average as the headline number. It stops at 12 reps, because estimates from very high rep sets are unreliable.

How to use the number

Most programs prescribe work as a percentage of 1RM. A rough guide many coaches use:

GoalTypical loadTypical reps
Strength80 to 90%2 to 6
Muscle size65 to 80%6 to 12
Technique and warm-up40 to 60%5 to 8

Make the estimate honest

  • Use a set taken close to failure with good form. An easy set underestimates.
  • Use a recent set. Your max from three months ago is history.
  • Recalculate every few weeks rather than retesting a true max. It is safer and costs no training days.
