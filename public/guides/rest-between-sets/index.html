Training basics

How long to rest between sets

Short answer: 2 to 3 minutes on big lifts, 1 to 2 minutes on smaller ones, and longer whenever your next set would otherwise suffer.

Updated September 2026

A practical starting point

Exercise typeRest
Heavy compound lifts (squat, bench, deadlift, press)2 to 3 minutes, up to 5 for very heavy sets
Accessory lifts (rows, curls, raises, machines)1 to 2 minutes
Conditioning circuits30 to 90 seconds

What the research suggests

For strength and muscle growth, longer rests tend to help because they let you do more reps with the same weight on later sets. A frequently cited study by Schoenfeld and colleagues (Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research, 2016) found trained men resting 3 minutes gained more strength and muscle than those resting 1 minute on the same program. Shorter rests can still work, but you usually need more sets to make up the difference.

That is why a very short rest is not a shortcut. If it costs you reps, you did less work, not more.

Signs your rest is too short

  • You lose two or more reps from one set to the next at the same weight.
  • You are still out of breath when you pick up the bar.
  • Form breaks down earlier than it did on the first set.

Using a rest timer well

A timer is useful as a floor, not a rule. Start the next set when the timer ends and you feel ready. On a heavy day, add 30 seconds without guilt. On accessories, you can trim it if reps are holding up.
