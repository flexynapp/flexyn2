When to take a deload week

A deload is a planned easier week. You keep training, just lighter or shorter, so fatigue can drop while fitness stays.

Updated September 2026

Why deload at all

Hard training builds fitness and fatigue at the same time. Fatigue hides fitness: you are stronger than your recent sessions show. A lighter week lets fatigue fall faster than fitness does, so you usually come back feeling better and lifting the same or more.

Signs you might need one

  • A lift has stalled or gone backwards for two or more sessions despite decent sleep and food.
  • Warm-up weights feel heavy.
  • Joints or tendons ache in a way that lingers between sessions.
  • Motivation to train has dropped for a week or more.
  • Your weekly volume has been well above your usual for several weeks in a row.

Many lifters simply plan one every 4 to 8 weeks instead of waiting for signs. Both approaches are common. Beginners making steady progress often need them less.

Three ways to run a deload

1. Cut the sets

Keep the same weights and exercises, do about half the sets. This keeps the movement practice and feels most like normal training.

2. Cut the weight

Keep the sets and reps, drop the load to roughly 60 to 70 percent of normal. Good when joints are the complaint.

3. Cut both, a little

Drop a set and take 10 to 20 percent off the bar. A middle ground if you are not sure.

What not to do

  • Do not use the week to test maxes or try new hard exercises.
  • Do not stop entirely unless you are sick or injured. Some training keeps skills and routine in place.
  • Do not panic if the first session back feels odd. It usually settles by the second.
